# D12 Ball 2.0

**D12 Ball 2.0 is the Charter ([living-rules.md](living-rules.md)) with the rules below: a
maneuver deck in place of how a coach chooses a maneuver (sections 1 to 3), roles that
belong to the formation rather than to the players, with no role abilities (section 4), and a
coin that settles tied rolls, with a maneuver costing exhaustion by its rank (section 5).**
Everything the Charter does not change here plays exactly as it says. D12 Ball 1.0 is the
Charter unchanged. The edition is chosen at setup, beside the mode, and either edition goes
with any of the three modes: the mode still decides which abilities are played (Law 18). Where
this file and the Charter disagree, in a 2.0 game, this file wins.

It is played on the Discord bot only for now. Its history is in [rules-log.md](rules-log.md).

## 1. The deck

**1.1** Each coach has their own deck of **eighteen maneuver cards**:

- **a.** **Twelve two-headed basic cards**, four of each rank. The top half of a two-headed
  card is the basic offensive maneuver of its rank and the bottom half, upside down, is the
  basic defensive maneuver of the same rank: the rank 1 card is Pass one way up and
  Deflect the other, rank 2 is Dribble and Steal, rank 3 is High Ball and Pressure. A
  two-headed card carries no role abilities. Across its middle is a band of matchups, read
  from each end by the half on that end: what that half beats, ties and loses to, by the
  other side's rank and basic maneuver -- the rank 1 card's Pass beats D3 (Pressure), ties
  D1 and loses to D2, and its Deflect beats O3, ties O1 and loses to O2.
- **b.** **Six advanced maneuvers**, one of each (Law 19.1): Pinpoint, Burst and Cross on
  offense, Clear, Intercept and Double Team on defense. An advanced card has one head and is
  played on its own side alone.

**1.2** Before kickoff each coach shuffles their deck and draws **a hand of five**. The hand is
secret. How many cards are in each coach's deck is public, and **each discard pile is face up**,
so both coaches see every card the other has played since the last shuffle.

## 2. Playing a card

**2.1** Wherever the Charter has a coach choose a maneuver (Law 6.2), they choose a card from
their hand instead, and play it on the side they are on: a two-headed card as the basic
maneuver its half for that side shows, an advanced card only on its own side. A hand always
holds a card either side can play, because only three of the eighteen are offense alone and
three defense alone.

**2.2** **There is no gambit declaration** (Law 19.3 does not apply): no coach holds a coin
to make a gambit with. Playing an advanced card is making a gambit, and it succeeds and fails on
rank as Law 19.4 says. The coin 2.0 does have settles tied rolls (section 5).

**2.3** An advanced card may be played against no challenge (Law 6.1.4), and then **resolves
as the basic maneuver on its rank**, since there is no card for it to win against.

**2.4** The cards are revealed together, as the Charter says; until both are down a coach may
change the card they chose.

## 3. After the reveal

**3.1** Each card played goes to its coach's **discard pile**, face up, and that coach draws
one card, back up to five. A coach who did not play a card -- the defense against no
challenge, either coach at a shot or a time out -- draws nothing.

**3.2** **When a coach draws the last card of their deck, they shuffle their discard pile to
make a new deck** at once.

**3.3** **At halftime each coach shuffles their hand and discard pile back into their deck and
draws a fresh hand of five.** Nothing else in the game draws, discards or shuffles a card.

## 4. Roles belong to the formation

**4.1** **A player has no role.** Each player keeps the offensive and defensive skill on their
card (Law 2.6.2), and in advanced mode an advanced skill still replaces one (Law 21.2). A squad
is still nine players (Law 2.6.1), and any of them may play any role.

**4.2** **Each zone has two roles, a unique one and a standard one.** The first player in a zone
takes its unique role and every other player in it the standard one:

| Zone | Unique role | Standard role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Outer zone before the team's own goal | Fullback | Defender |
| Midfield | Playmaker | Midfielder |
| Outer zone before the goal the team attacks | Striker | Winger |

So a formation (Law 14.7) decides the roles by how many it puts in each zone, read from the
coach's own goal forward:

| Formation | Outer zone before their own goal | Midfield | Outer zone before the goal they attack |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2-2-2 | Fullback, Defender | Playmaker, Midfielder | Striker, Winger |
| 2-3-1 | Fullback, Defender | Playmaker, Midfielder, Midfielder | Striker |
| 1-3-2 | Fullback | Playmaker, Midfielder, Midfielder | Striker, Winger |
| 3-2-1 | Fullback, Defender, Defender | Playmaker, Midfielder | Striker |
| 1-2-3 | Fullback | Playmaker, Midfielder | Striker, Winger, Winger |

Every formation fields one Fullback, one Playmaker and one Striker, and none, one or two
Defenders, Midfielders and Wingers.

**4.3** **A side is set up in three steps**, at the standard deal, after a formation change and
in any Coaching Choice:

- **a.** **The coach chooses a formation**, which says how many players stand in each zone (Law
  14.7) and so which roles it has (4.2).
- **b.** **The coach assigns players to the roles.** Assigning a unique role fixes that player's
  space:
  - the **Fullback** stands on the last space before their own goal: space −3, −4 or −5 for
    home, and 3, 4 or 5 for the visitors;
  - the **Playmaker** stands on their own kickoff space (Law 2.4), the space of midfield nearest
    the middle: space 0 on boards 7 and 9, and on board 10 space −1 for home and 1 for the
    visitors -- so every arrangement covers its own kickoff space (Law 14.8.1) with its
    Playmaker;
  - the **Striker** stands on the last space before the goal they attack: space 3, 4 or 5 for
    home, and −3, −4 or −5 for the visitors.
- **c.** **The coach gives each player in a standard role one of their zone's other spaces**, a
  player a space. No formation puts more players in a zone than it has spaces, so nobody
  stacks.

**4.4** **The Playmaker always takes the kickoff.** A restart from a side's kickoff space -- the
kickoff of each half (Law 3.4, 16.4.4), after a goal (5.5.2) and after an own goal conceded
(11.4.2) -- leaves the ball with that side's Playmaker, as a carrier (Law 4.2.2), rather than
with nobody in particular (4.2.3).

**4.5** **For your first game, each team has a recommended arrangement**, and the standard deal
sets it up, making all three choices of 4.3 for the coach. It starts the same six players as D12
Ball 1.0's (Law 3.2), in 2-2-2, each in the role they hold in 1.0:

| Team | Fullback | Defender | Playmaker | Midfielder | Striker | Winger |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Orange | Goopkeeper | Blazebulk | Sizzifizik | Spectra | Acidel | Flickerwing |
| Teal | Bulwark | Inferno | Ozul | Strider | Pulsar | Zenith |
| Purple | Hellguard | Dravox | Emberdash | Glompex | Tachyon | Quantor |
| Slime | Umbrik | Flux | Gearclaw | Scorchit | Brightburn | Shpritz |
| Fire Demons | Hellguard | Blazebulk | Sizzifizik | Scorchit | Brightburn | Flickerwing |
| Cyborgs | Bulwark | Voltus | Synapse | Strider | Tachyon | Quantor |
| Telekinetics | Umbrik | Dravox | Quillon | Spectra | Noxar | Zenith |
| Oozes | Goopkeeper | Gurgoth | Ozul | Glompex | Acidel | Shpritz |

The Defender and the Winger stand on their zone's space furthest from its unique role, and the
Midfielder on the space behind the Playmaker, toward their own goal. On boards 7 and 9 those are
the 1.0 deal's own spaces; on board 10 the Midfielder stands behind the Playmaker, on space −2
for home and 2 for the visitors, where 1.0 puts the Midfielder on the kickoff space. The other
three start on the bench, as in 1.0.

*Tip:* it's a good idea to keep players with higher offensive skills forward and vice versa,
which is what this arrangement does. As you gain experience with the game, feel free to change
things around!

**4.6** **In a Coaching Choice** (Law 14.2) a player's role decides how they may move:

- **a.** A **formation change** is the coach's own choice of roles and spaces under the new
  formation, made as 4.3 says. Nobody is dealt by defensive skill (Law 14.7.3 does not apply).
- **b.** A **substitution** brings the player on into the outgoing player's role, with their
  zone and their space.
- **c.** A **zone assignment** trades two fielded players' roles, and their zones, cards and
  meeples with them -- two players in the same zone included. It is how a coach puts a different
  player in a unique role.
- **d.** **Space positioning** never changes a role. A player in a unique role may not move to
  another space. A player in a standard role may move to an empty space in their zone, or to one
  held by a teammate in a standard role, the two swapping places; never onto a unique role's
  space.
- **e.** **An Ooze bends 4.3c and 4.6d** in standard and advanced mode, because Spreadable (Law
  20.5.4) counts it as 0 toward its zone's occupancy. An Ooze in a standard role may be placed
  on any space in its zone, sharing it with anybody, a unique role included. An Ooze in a unique
  role is still locked onto its space, but a teammate in a standard role may be placed on that
  space to share it. A zone assignment or a substitution that puts somebody who is not an Ooze
  into that unique role still stands, and the teammate sharing the space moves off it to an empty
  space in their zone, for nothing; where more than one is empty, their coach chooses which, and
  may not finish the Coaching Choice until they have.

**4.7** **There are no role abilities.** Nothing a role adds in the Charter applies in a 2.0
game -- neither the six abilities of Law 2.6.2 nor the role lines on the advanced maneuvers
(Law 19). So, in every mode:

- **a.** a High Ball is thrown 2 or 3 spaces (6.7.2), a Deflect sends the ball 1 space back
  (6.8.3), a Clear 3 (19.8.4), a Cross is picked out 0, 1 or 3 spaces (19.7.4), and a failed
  Cross goes back 1 space off a Deflect and 3 off a Clear (19.7.7);
- **b.** a won Pressure never steals the ball (6.10.4), so it always leaves the ball with the
  handler who was shoved back (4.2.2e), and is never a turnover (12.1);
- **c.** nobody adds 3 to a skill test for their own Pass, Steal, Pinpoint or Intercept (6.4.2a,
  19.5.4, 19.9.4);
- **d.** a Dribble advances 1 space (6.6.4) and a Burst up to 4 (19.6.3);
- **e.** **a completed Pass or Pinpoint never sets up a scoring opportunity** (8.2.1, 19.5.3);
  the other three set-ups of Law 8.2 stand;
- **f.** a score attempt off a set-up adds nothing for the shooter's role (8.3.1, 15.4.3).

**4.8** A special ability (Law 21) belongs to the player, not the role, and is still played in
advanced mode. Where Law 21.1.2 plays it beside the player's role ability, in 2.0 there is no
role ability to play it beside.

*Note.* For now a role carries no ability in 2.0: it decides which zone a player is in, where
the three unique roles stand, who may be moved in a Coaching Choice, and who takes the kickoff.

## 5. Ties and the coin

**5.1** **The coin settles every tied roll** -- a maneuver's skill test (Law 6.4) and a contest
for the ball, a High Ball contest included (Law 10.2, 10.4). Nothing is rolled again, and so
nothing is paid for a re-roll (Law 6.4.4, 10.2.2 and the re-roll in Law 15.1.1 do not apply).

- **a.** **While the coin shows Fortune the offense wins a tied roll; while it shows Doom the
  defense does.**
- **b.** In a skill test the offense is the handler's side. In a contest for the ball it is the
  side that last held the ball -- the side that adds its offensive skill (Law 10.2.1).
- **c.** A tie on the cards is still a skill test (Law 6.3.1), and a tie a player loses for being
  injured is still lost with nothing rolled (Law 15.4.2a). Only an equal total on the dice goes to
  the coin. A score attempt (Law 5.2.3) and a shootout test (Law 17.4.3) are not settled by it.

**5.2** **The coin is flipped at the start of the game**, before the first turn, and lands on
Fortune or Doom.

**5.3** **Whenever an advanced card is played** -- by either coach, challenged or not -- **the
coin is tossed again once that maneuver is fully resolved**, every contest it leads to
included, and its new face holds until the next maneuver an advanced card is played in. So a
tied roll in that maneuver is settled by the face the coin showed when the cards were revealed.

**5.4** **A maneuver costs exhaustion by its rank.** Every player who plays a card exhausts its
rank -- 1, 2 or 3, an advanced card the rank it shares with its basic maneuver (Law 19.1.2) --
when the cards are revealed, win or lose, and the handler of an unchallenged maneuver too.
**Entering a skill test costs nothing** (Law 6.4.1 does not apply), except that a test
Scorchit forces in advanced mode still costs Scorchit 2, the price of the ability (Law 21.3.8),
and their opponent nothing. Merging into a roll still costs 1 (Law 20.5). So the first row of Law
15.1.1 reads:

| What costs tokens | How many |
| --- | --- |
| Playing a maneuver card | its rank, to the player who plays it |

**5.5** The tokens a card costs count toward the injury checks of the skill test it leads to
(Law 15.3.2). A maneuver settled on the cards owes no injury check, however many tokens it cost
(Law 15.3.3). An injured player still never exhausts (Law 15.1.3).
