# D12 Ball 2.0 -- the maneuver deck

**D12 Ball 2.0 is the Charter ([living-rules.md](living-rules.md)) with the rules below in
place of how a coach chooses a maneuver.** Everything the Charter does not change here plays
exactly as it says. D12 Ball 1.0 is the Charter unchanged. The edition is chosen at setup,
beside the mode, and either edition goes with any of the three modes: the mode still decides
which abilities are played (Law 18). Where this file and the Charter disagree, in a 2.0 game,
this file wins.

It is played on the Discord bot only for now. Its history is in [rules-log.md](rules-log.md).

## 1. The deck

**1.1** Each coach has their own deck of **fifteen maneuver cards**:

- **a.** **Nine two-headed basic cards**, three of each rank. The top half of a two-headed
  card is the basic offensive maneuver of its rank and the bottom half, upside down, is the
  basic defensive maneuver of the same rank: the rank 1 card is Low Pass one way up and
  Deflect the other, rank 2 is Dribble and Steal, rank 3 is High Pass and Pressure. A
  two-headed card carries no role abilities. Across its middle is a band of matchups, read
  from each end by the half on that end: what that half beats, ties and loses to, by the
  other side's rank and maneuvers -- the rank 1 card's Low Pass beats D3 (Pressure, Double
  Team), ties D1 and loses to D2, and its Deflect beats O3, ties O1 and loses to O2.
- **b.** **Six advanced maneuvers**, one of each (Law 19.1): Pinpoint, Burst and Cross on
  offense, Clear, Intercept and Double Team on defense. An advanced card has one head and is
  played on its own side alone.

**1.2** Before kickoff each coach shuffles their deck and draws **a hand of four**. The hand is
secret. How many cards are in each coach's deck is public, and **each discard pile is face up**,
so both coaches see every card the other has played since the last shuffle.

## 2. Playing a card

**2.1** Wherever the Charter has a coach choose a maneuver (Law 6.2), they choose a card from
their hand instead, and play it on the side they are on: a two-headed card as the basic
maneuver its half for that side shows, an advanced card only on its own side. A hand always
holds a card either side can play, because only three of the fifteen are offense alone and
three defense alone.

**2.2** **There is no coin and no gambit declaration** (Law 19.3 does not apply). Playing an
advanced card is making a gambit, and it succeeds and fails on rank as Law 19.4 says.

**2.3** An advanced card may be played against no challenge (Law 6.1.4), and then **resolves
as the basic maneuver on its rank**, since there is no card for it to win against.

**2.4** The cards are revealed together, as the Charter says; until both are down a coach may
change the card they chose.

## 3. After the reveal

**3.1** Each card played goes to its coach's **discard pile**, face up, and that coach draws
one card, back up to four. A coach who did not play a card -- the defense against no
challenge, either coach at a shot or a time out -- draws nothing.

**3.2** **When a coach draws the last card of their deck, they shuffle their discard pile to
make a new deck** at once.

**3.3** **At halftime each coach shuffles their hand and discard pile back into their deck and
draws a fresh hand of four.** Nothing else in the game draws, discards or shuffles a card.
