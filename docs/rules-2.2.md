# D12 Ball 2.2

**D12 Ball 2.2 is the Charter ([living-rules.md](living-rules.md)) with the rules below: a
deck of twelve split maneuver cards in place of how a coach chooses a maneuver, each card an
offensive basic maneuver on its top half and the defensive one of the same rank on its bottom
half, each half with a role special, and a hand of three (sections 1 to 3, and 7); roles that belong to the formation rather than
to the players, whose only abilities are the cards' specials (section 4); a coin whose face
settles every tie and is tossed again after it, with moving on the board costing exhaustion a space (section 5); and no
shooting range (section 6). There are no advanced maneuvers.**
Everything the Charter does not change here plays exactly as it says. D12 Ball 1.0 is the
Charter unchanged. The edition is chosen at setup, beside the mode, and either edition goes
with any of the three modes: the mode still decides which abilities are played (Law 18). Where
this file and the Charter disagree, in a 2.2 game, this file wins.

2.2 replaced D12 Ball 2.1 on 2026-11-01: 2.1's twenty-four single-faced cards are 2.2's
twenty-four halves, printed two to a card; the hand is three cards where 2.1's was five; and
2.1's flip -- a card played as the other side's maneuver for an extra card -- is gone, since every
card now plays on either side. The rest of 2.1 is 2.2's unchanged. 2.1 itself had replaced D12
Ball 2.0's two-headed cards and its six advanced maneuvers on 2026-10-31, keeping the rest of
2.0 -- the roles, the moving costs, no range. 2.1's rules as they stood are in
[archive/rules-2.1/](../archive/rules-2.1/). 2.2 is played on the Discord bot only for now.
Its history is in [rules-log.md](rules-log.md).

## 1. The deck

**1.1** Each coach has their own deck of **twelve split maneuver cards**. **A card's top half
is an offensive maneuver and its bottom half the defensive maneuver of the same rank, with the
same number** (section 7), so there are four cards a rank:

- **a.** P1 to P4 Pass over F1 to F4 Deflect; B1 to B4 Dribble over S1 to S4 Steal; H1 to H4
  High Ball over R1 to R4 Pressure. P2/F2 is one card: Pass and Run on offense, Follow on
  defense. Each half is called by its index, as a card was before.
- **b.** **Every half plays its maneuver**, with the Charter's rank (Law 6.3) and the rank's
  own effect, which is the same on the four halves of that rank (section 7). What tells the
  four apart is each half's **special**: a role interaction, "If the player is a Fullback or a
  Defender, ...", which applies only when the player making the maneuver holds one of the
  roles it names (2.2).
- **c.** **The four halves of a rank name all six roles between them, each exactly once**, so
  whatever role a player holds, one half of each rank has a special for them. A rank's four
  offensive halves are on the same four cards as the four defensive halves of that rank, so
  each of those cards names roles twice -- once a half.
- **d.** **There are no advanced maneuvers** (Law 19 does not apply).

**1.2** Before kickoff each coach shuffles their deck and draws **a hand of three**. The hand is
secret. How many cards are in each coach's deck is public, and **each discard pile is face up**,
so both coaches see every card the other has played since the last shuffle.

## 2. Playing a card

**2.1** Wherever the Charter has a coach choose a maneuver (Law 6.2), they choose a card from
their hand instead. **A card plays the half of the coach's side** -- its top half on offense,
its bottom half on defense -- as that half's maneuver, with its special (2.2).

**2.2** **A special applies when the player making the maneuver holds one of the roles it
names** -- the handler on offense, the challenger on defense (Law 6.1) -- in the role the
formation gives them (section 4). A special adds to the card's maneuver or replaces part of it,
as section 7 says, and is the only role ability in 2.2 (4.7). **Whatever a card says a player
"may" do is optional**: the coach chooses whether to.

**2.3** **Every card plays on either side**, as the half of that side, and a card is played
alone. A hand of three may not hold every rank; a coach plays what they hold.

**2.4** **There is no gambit declaration and no gambit coin** (Law 19.3 does not apply). The
coin 2.2 does have settles ties (section 5).

**2.5** The cards are revealed together, as the Charter says; until both are down a coach may
change the card they chose. Against no challenge (Law 6.1.4)
the offense still plays a card, and its special applies to the handler.

## 3. After the reveal

**3.1** Each card played goes to its coach's **discard pile**, face up, both halves together,
and that coach draws back up to three. A coach who did not play a card -- the
defense against no challenge, either coach at a shot or a time out -- draws nothing.

**3.2** **When a coach draws the last card of their deck, they shuffle their discard pile to
make a new deck** at once.

**3.3** **At halftime each coach shuffles their hand and discard pile back into their deck and
draws a fresh hand of three.** Nothing else in the game draws, discards or shuffles a card.

## 4. Roles belong to the formation

**4.1** **A player has no role.** Each player keeps the offensive and defensive skill on their
card (Law 2.6.2), and in advanced mode an advanced skill still replaces one (Law 21.2). A squad
is still nine players (Law 2.6.1), and any of them may play any role.

**4.2** **Each zone has two roles, a unique one and a standard one.** One player in a zone is
assigned its unique role (4.3a) and every other player in it holds the standard one:

| Zone | Unique role | Standard role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Outer zone before the team's own goal | Fullback | Defender |
| Midfield | Playmaker | Midfielder |
| Outer zone before the goal the team attacks | Striker | Winger |

**There are no formations** (Law 14.7 does not apply): a side's shape is whatever its
arrangement (4.3) makes, read from the coach's own goal forward -- 2-2-2, 3-1-2 or, on board 10,
1-4-1 alike. Every arrangement fields one Fullback, one Playmaker and one Striker, and in each
zone as many players in its standard role as its spaces leave room for.

**4.3** **A side is arranged in two steps.** The standard deal makes both for each coach at the
start of the game (4.5); after that a coach changes the arrangement their side stands in, in a
Coaching Choice (4.6a), and what they leave alone stays as it is:

- **a.** **The coach assigns players to the three unique roles**, the Fullback, the Playmaker and
  the Striker. Each stands on their role's space:
  - the **Fullback** on the last space before their own goal: space −3, −4 or −5 for home, and
    3, 4 or 5 for the visitors;
  - the **Playmaker** on their own kickoff space (Law 2.4), the space of midfield nearest the
    middle: space 0 on boards 7 and 9, and on board 10 space −1 for home and 1 for the visitors
    -- so every arrangement covers its own kickoff space (Law 14.8.1) with its Playmaker;
  - the **Striker** on the last space before the goal they attack: space 3, 4 or 5 for home,
    and −3, −4 or −5 for the visitors.
- **b.** **The coach places three more players**, a player a space, each on any space of the
  field in any zone that the three unique roles do not stand on and nobody else has been placed
  on. Each takes the **standard role of the zone they are placed in**. Nobody stacks, which an
  Ooze bends (4.6e).

**No zone ever holds more players than it has spaces, Oozes included.** An Ooze may share a
space, but the players it shares with still count toward the zone's spaces -- so a zone with an
Ooze sharing always has a space standing empty. This holds for every arrangement, at the
standard deal and in every Coaching Choice.
- **c.** **Before kickoff the six are chosen from the whole squad of nine**, and the three left
  out start on the bench: a coach rearranging before kickoff may put a bench player in any
  fielded player's place. In any later Coaching Choice the arrangement is made from the six on
  the field; only a substitution (4.6b) brings anybody on.

**4.4** **The Playmaker always takes the kickoff.** A restart from a side's kickoff space -- the
kickoff of each half (Law 3.4, 16.4.4), after a goal (5.5.2) and after an own goal conceded
(11.4.2) -- leaves the ball with that side's Playmaker, as a carrier (Law 4.2.2), rather than
with nobody in particular (4.2.3).

**4.5** **For your first game, each team has a recommended arrangement**, and the standard deal
sets it up, making both steps of 4.3 for the coach, who may arrange the side again before
kickoff. It starts the same six players as D12 Ball 1.0's (Law 3.2), in 2-2-2, each in the role
they hold in 1.0:

| Team | Fullback | Defender | Playmaker | Midfielder | Striker | Winger |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Orange | Goopkeeper | Blazebulk | Sizzifizik | Spectra | Acidel | Flickerwing |
| Teal | Bulwark | Inferno | Ozul | Strider | Pulsar | Zenith |
| Purple | Hellguard | Dravox | Emberdash | Glompex | Tachyon | Quantor |
| Slime | Umbrik | Flux | Gearclaw | Scorchit | Brightburn | Shpritz |
| Fire Demons | Hellguard | Blazebulk | Sizzifizik | Scorchit | Brightburn | Flickerwing |
| Cyborgs | Bulwark | Voltus | Synapse | Strider | Tachyon | Quantor |
| Telekinetics | Umbrik | Dravox | Quillon | Spectra | Noxar | Zenith |
| Oozes | Goopkeeper | Gurgoth | Ozul | Glompex | Acidel | Shpritz |

The Defender and the Winger stand on their zone's space furthest from its unique role, and the
Midfielder on the space behind the Playmaker, toward their own goal. On boards 7 and 9 those are
the 1.0 deal's own spaces; on board 10 the Midfielder stands behind the Playmaker, on space −2
for home and 2 for the visitors, where 1.0 puts the Midfielder on the kickoff space. The other
three start on the bench, as in 1.0.

*Tip:* it's a good idea to keep players with higher offensive skills forward and vice versa,
which is what this arrangement does. As you gain experience with the game, feel free to change
things around!

**4.6** **In a Coaching Choice** (Law 14.2) a coach moves their side by rearranging it and
brings players on by substitution, and nothing else:

- **a.** **Rearranging** takes the place of a formation change: starting from the side as it
  stands, the coach may put a different player in a unique role, move a player in a standard
  role to any space with room for them -- in any zone, taking that zone's standard role -- or
  have two players trade places and roles, as often as they like, into whatever shape that
  makes. Nobody is dealt by defensive skill (Law 14.7.3 does not apply).
- **b.** A **substitution** brings the player on into the outgoing player's role, with their
  zone and their space.
- **c.** **There is no zone assignment and no space positioning** (Law 14.2.1): rearranging's
  trades and moves (4.6a) do what both did.
- **d.** **A unique role holds its space.** Nobody moves a Fullback, a Playmaker or a Striker
  off their role's space except by rearranging the role onto somebody else, and nobody but an
  Ooze is moved onto it (4.6e).
- **e.** **An Ooze bends 4.3b** in standard and advanced mode, because Spreadable (Law 20.5.4)
  counts it as 0 toward its zone's occupancy. An Ooze may be placed on any space, sharing it
  with anybody, a unique role included, and a teammate may be placed on an Ooze's space, an Ooze
  in a unique role included; either way its zone still holds no more players than it has
  spaces. A substitution that puts somebody who is not an Ooze into an Ooze's unique role still
  stands, and a teammate sharing that space moves off it, for nothing: to the one empty space of
  their zone where there is only one, and otherwise wherever their coach rearranges them (4.6a)
  -- and the coach may not finish the Coaching Choice until they have.

**4.7** **There are no role abilities but the cards' specials** (2.2, section 7). Nothing the
six roles add in the Charter (Law 2.6.2) applies in a 2.2 game; where a special does what a
Charter ability did, it does it on its own card alone. So, in every mode, but for a special:

- **a.** a High Ball is thrown 3 spaces (7.3) and a Deflect sends the ball 1 space back (7.4a);
- **b.** a won Pressure never steals the ball (6.10.4), so it leaves the ball with the handler
  who was shoved back (4.2.2e), and is not a turnover (12.1);
- **c.** nobody adds 3 to a skill test for their own Pass or Steal (6.4.2a);
- **d.** a Dribble advances 1 space (6.6.4);
- **e.** **a completed Pass never sets up a scoring opportunity** (8.2.1); the other three
  set-ups of Law 8.2 stand;
- **f.** a score attempt off a set-up adds nothing for the shooter's role (8.3.1, 15.4.3).

**4.8** A special ability (Law 21) belongs to the player, not the role, and is still played in
advanced mode. Where Law 21.1.2 plays it beside the player's role ability, in 2.2 there is no
role ability to play it beside.

**4.9** **A new play is a run back.** There is no arrangement to reset to: after a new play (Law
12.5) players run back as after a steal, with two differences.

- **a.** **Everybody runs back, on both sides**, whether or not they are already in their own
  zone, and nobody stays for holding the ball -- it is dead. Each player in a unique role goes
  straight to their role's space (4.3b), and each other player to an open space of their own
  zone -- one no teammate has already been placed on -- their coach choosing which, home's
  coach first. Since no zone holds more players than it has spaces, there is always one.
- **b.** **It costs nothing** (5.4a).
- **c.** **The Coaching Choice comes after the run back**, on the side as it then stands.
- **d.** **A time out (Law 13.1.2) and halftime send the unique roles back alone.** As each
  coach's Coaching Choice opens, their Fullback, Playmaker and Striker go straight back onto
  their own spaces, for nothing, and everyone else stays where they stand, keeping their role.
  The coach then rearranges the side (4.6a) and may not finish the Coaching Choice until it
  stands in an arrangement 4.3 allows -- nobody sharing a space but an Ooze, no zone holding
  more players than it has spaces, and **every player in the zone of their role**. A player
  standing outside it is either moved back to a space of their zone, keeping their role, or
  given a new role: the standard role of the zone they are moved to, or of the zone they stand
  in where the coach leaves them there. Until the side is in place, each change must leave
  fewer players out of place.
- **e.** **After a steal (Law 12.4)** the run back is the Charter's: everyone outside their own
  zone and one of any stack while their zone has an open space, a player in a unique role
  included, runs back to an open space of their zone, never the player holding the ball, at a
  token a space.

So a new play and a steal are one run back: a steal leaves the ball's holder where they are,
moves only those out of place and charges them, and a new play moves everybody for nothing.

*Note.* A role decides which card's special a player plays (2.2), which zone a player is in,
where the three unique roles stand, who may be moved in a Coaching Choice, who takes the
kickoff, and where a player goes in a new play's run back.

## 5. Ties, the coin and moving

**5.1** **The coin settles every tie** -- a maneuver's skill test (Law 6.4), a contest for the
ball, a High Ball contest included (Law 10.2, 10.4), and a score attempt (Law 5.2.3). Nothing
is rolled again, so nothing is paid for a re-roll (Law 6.4.4, 10.2.2 and the re-roll in Law
15.1.1 do not apply), and a tied shot is not a goal by itself.

- **a.** **Fortune: the offense wins the tie; Doom: the defense does.** At a score attempt the
  offense is the attack: Fortune scores, Doom does not.
- **b.** In a skill test the offense is the handler's side. In a contest for the ball it is the
  side that last held the ball -- the side that adds its offensive skill (Law 10.2.1).
- **c.** A tie on the cards is still a skill test (Law 6.3.1), and a tie a player loses for being
  injured is still lost with nothing rolled (Law 15.4.2a). Only an equal total on the dice goes to
  the coin.

**5.2** **The coin starts on the face the toss landed on** (Law 3.1.2): the coin that decided
who chooses home or the visitors is not flipped again before the first turn, and that face settles
the first tie. **After every tie it settles, it is tossed again**, and its new face holds until
the next tie -- so both coaches always know how the next tie will go. The face is public, shown
on every turn.

**5.3** **A tied shootout test is no goal for either side** (Law 17.4.3); the coin neither
settles it nor is tossed for it.

**5.4** **Moving costs exhaustion: a player who moves on the board during play exhausts 1 for
every space they travel**, whoever moved them and whichever card did -- the handler of a won
Dribble (Law 6.6.2), the passer stepping forward after P2's Pass (6.5.3), the handler a Pressure
shoves back and the challenger who moves onto their space (6.10.2), the thief a Steal carries
off with the ball (6.9.2), and the player who follows the ball after F2's Deflect or R2's
Pressure (7.4), as well as every move the Charter already charges a space -- a player sent to
the ball (9.4) and a player running back after a steal (12.4.3).

- **a.** **Two kinds of move are free**: the run back after a new play (4.9), and every move a
  Coaching Choice makes -- a rearrangement, a substitution and stepping off a unique role's
  space (Law 14; section 4).
- **b.** **An ability or a card that prices its own move keeps that price**: Emberdash's Burst
  costs nothing and so, in 2.2, does their Dribble (Law 21.3.4); a Strider runs back for at most
  1 (21.4.4); Quantor's run and Glompex's step onto the ball cost what their abilities say
  (21.4.5, 21.6.1).
- **c.** **Playing a card costs nothing, and so does entering a skill test** (Law 6.4.1 does not
  apply), except that a test Scorchit forces in advanced mode still costs Scorchit 2, the price
  of the ability (Law 21.3.8), and their opponent nothing. Merging into a roll still costs 1
  (Law 20.5).

So the first three rows of Law 15.1.1 -- entering a skill test, being sent to the ball and
running back -- are one:

| What costs tokens | How many |
| --- | --- |
| Moving on the board during play, but for a reset or a Coaching Choice | 1 per space traveled |

**5.5** **Every score attempt exhausts the shooter 1, after the roll, whether it is off a set-up
or not** -- an ordinary turn's shot as much as a scoring opportunity's (Law 8.3.1, for every
shot; Law 5 charges a plain one nothing). It owes no injury check, as no shot does.

## 6. No shooting range

**6.1** **There is no shooting range: a team may shoot from anywhere on the field** (Law 2.3
does not apply). Wherever the Charter asks whether the ball is in range, in 2.2 it is:

- **a.** A score attempt is offered on every turn (Law 1.3, 4.2b, 4.3.1, 5.1.1).
- **b.** The ball speed modifier is added to a shot from wherever it is taken (Law 5.2.2,
  7.4.1).
- **c.** Every set-up's scoring opportunity may be taken wherever the ball is -- a 2-space High
  Ball's, Vorix's and Zytheris's (Law 6.7, 8.1.2, 21.5.6, 21.5.8).

A shot from further out is harder without a range to forbid it: every defender between the ball
and the goal still adds half their defensive skill (Law 5.3.2).

**6.2** **A time out may be called on any turn the side has the ball**, under the other two
conditions as they stand: the side has not yet taken its time out this half, and last possession
has not been declared (Law 13.2.1b and c). Being out of range is no longer one of them (Law
4.3.3, 13.1.1 and 13.2.1a do not apply).

## 7. The maneuver cards

**7.1** **2.2 plays the `maneuvers 2.1` tab of the sheet** -- 2.1's cards, which are 2.2's
halves: four halves a basic maneuver, each
with its rank's effect and a special of its own, printed two to a card -- the offensive half on
top, the defensive half of the same rank and number below it (1.1). Who wins a maneuver is the Charter's (Law
6.3): rank alone decides, so O1 beats D3, ties D1 and loses to D2, and so on round the cycle.
Every card costs 1 time but the High Balls, which cost 2 (Law 16.2).

| Index | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| P (Pass, O1) | Pass | Pass and Run | Pinpoint | Reset |
| B (Dribble, O2) | Dribble | Advance | Spin | Drive |
| H (High Ball, O3) | High Ball | Long shot | Set up | Vertical Ball |
| F (Deflect, D1) | Deflect | Follow | Boot | Block |
| S (Steal, D2) | Steal | Pickpocket | My Turf! | Toss |
| R (Pressure, D3) | Pressure | Control | Stay Close | Stress |

A half is named by its index: P3 is a Pass, whatever its name says. A card is named by its two
halves, P3/F3.

**7.2** **Pass and Dribble.**

- **a.** *Pass:* the ball goes to the nearest teammate ahead of it or behind it, at any
  distance -- a teammate on the ball's own space counting as the nearest behind (Law 6.5.2) --
  and ball speed rises by 1. The passer does not move. A pass with no teammate to reach rolls 2
  spaces forward and settles where it lands (Law 6.5.5).
  - **P1** *Winger:* a completed Pass may set up a scoring opportunity for its receiver (Law
    6.5.6).
  - **P2** *Midfielder or Striker:* once the ball has gone the passer may move 1 space forward
    (Law 6.5.3), at its cost (5.4).
  - **P3** *Playmaker:* the pass may instead go to any teammate up to 2 spaces away, either way.
  - **P4** *Fullback or Defender:* the pass may instead go to the side's Playmaker, wherever
    they are.
- **b.** *Dribble:* the handler and the ball move 1 space forward, and ball speed rises by 2.
  - **B1** *Striker:* the handler may then attempt scoring, as a scoring opportunity (Law 8.1.1),
    wherever they are (section 6) -- but not one off a set-up. It exhausts the shooter 1 after
    the roll, as every score attempt does (5.5).
  - **B2** *Midfielder or Playmaker:* the handler may advance 1 more space.
  - **B3** *Fullback or Defender:* the handler then changes the ball's speed by up to their
    defensive skill, in either direction.
  - **B4** *Winger:* a teammate sharing the space the handler moved to may take the ball -- their
    coach chooses who handles it next (Law 4.2.3).

**7.3** **High Ball.** The ball is thrown 3 spaces forward; a teammate there has to win the High
Ball contest to keep it (Law 10.4), and having kept it may take a scoring opportunity (Law 8). A
throw that reaches the goal zone is the Charter's (Law 6.7.4).

- **H1** *Fullback or Defender:* it may be thrown 4 instead.
- **H2** *Playmaker:* it may go to the side's Striker instead, wherever they are, still
  contested.
- **H3** *Midfielder:* it may be thrown 2 instead, received with no contest, and may set up a
  scoring opportunity -- the Charter's High Ball of 2.
- **H4** *Winger or Striker:* it may go 0 or 1 spaces instead, still a High Ball: contested,
  and having kept it the receiver may take a scoring opportunity. At 0 it comes down on the
  passer's own space and the passer is its receiver -- the one High Ball a passer receives; at 1
  a teammate there receives it, and with nobody there it settles where it lands (Law 10.1).

**7.4** **The defensive cards.**

- **a.** *Deflect:* the ball moves 1 space back and its speed drops by 1. It lands as the
  Charter's Deflect does (Law 6.8.4), and where it reaches the goal zone it sets up a scoring
  opportunity for the player who played it, as Law 6.8.5 does -- a set-up they take, not one
  they may let go (the author, 2026-10-31).
  - **F1** *Fullback:* it moves a space further (Law 6.8.3).
  - **F2** *Defender or Midfielder:* the player who played it may follow the ball onto the space
    it lands on, at its cost (5.4).
  - **F3** *Winger or Striker:* the player who played it may take a scoring opportunity wherever
    the ball lands, not only in the goal zone, shooting from there -- possession flips for it and
    the speed resets, as Law 6.8.5's does; let go, the ball lands by the ordinary rule.
  - **F4** *Playmaker:* the ball goes nowhere and is contested on the spot, by the handler and
    the player who played it (Law 10.2); the speed is left alone.
- **b.** *Steal:* a turnover; the speed resets to 1 (Law 7.3), and the player who played it and
  the ball move 1 space back. Nobody changes the speed after (Law 6.9.3 does not apply). The
  ball speed modifier is added to its skill test (Law 7.4.1).
  - **S1** *Playmaker:* once everyone is back, they change the ball's speed by up to their
    offensive skill.
  - **S2** *Midfielder:* they do not move back: the ball is turned over where it is.
  - **S3** *Fullback or Defender:* they add 3 to this maneuver's skill test.
  - **S4** *Winger or Striker:* they stay in place and play the ball to their nearest teammate
    behind them -- one sharing their space counting as behind; with nobody behind them they keep
    it.
- **c.** *Pressure:* the handler and the ball go 1 space back and the player who played it moves
  1 forward onto that space; reaching the handler's own goal zone risks an own goal (Law 6.10).
  - **R1** *Defender:* a turnover, and nobody moves.
  - **R2** *Fullback or Playmaker:* the ball goes back to the handler's nearest teammate behind
    them -- one sharing their space counting as behind -- and the player who played it may follow
    it onto that space. With nobody behind the handler it reaches their own goal zone and risks an
    own goal (Law 11).
  - **R3** *Midfielder:* the handler also exhausts 1.
  - **R4** *Striker or Winger:* the ball goes nowhere and is contested in place, by the handler
    and the player who played it (Law 10.2).
- **d.** Every move these cards make on the board costs its spaces (5.4).

**7.5** Where the tab leaves a card's `Mode` or `Time` blank, the card is basic and costs 1 (the
author, 2026-10-27).
