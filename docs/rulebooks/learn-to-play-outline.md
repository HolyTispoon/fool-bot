# D12 Ball -- Learn to Play: outline

Sixteen letter pages, eight spreads, basic mode only. Every rule on a
page cites the Charter Law it comes from (numbers per
[charter-outline.md](charter-outline.md)); every figure is sketched in
[figures/](figures/) and shown here where it goes. Read
[plan.md](plan.md) for the principles this is written to.

The sketches are for approving **what each picture shows**. They come
off the bot's own board renderer (the positions are real match states),
with the arrows and numbered notes added; the final art is a later
milestone. The sample teams are purple (home) and teal (visitors), and
the players named are the ones the standard deal fields for those
teams -- the artist's version can use whichever kits the printed
components ship with.

---

## Page 1 -- Cover

*Art needed.* Two coaches across a table, the field board between them,
a d12 rolling. Title, and the one line that sets the two books up:

> This book teaches you to play. The Charter settles every question.
> If they disagree, the Charter is right and this book has left
> something out on purpose.

## Page 2 -- What is D12 Ball, and what is in the box

- Three sentences: two coaches, nine players each, thirty minutes on a
  clock; you win on goals; a level game goes to the shootout, so
  somebody always wins. (Law 1)
- **Components** -- *art needed*: the field board, the jumbotron, the
  two team boards, the eighteen player cards, the six maneuver cards
  (twelve in the box; the basic six are marked), the d12 ball, four
  d12s, the exhaustion tokens, the two condition markers, the coin.
  Each labelled; a caption under the twelve cards says the six with
  the gambit band are advanced mode and stay in the box for now.
- A sidebar: *Basic and advanced mode.* One sentence each, and "this
  book teaches basic mode; the appendix on page 15 says what advanced
  mode adds."

## Spread 1 (pages 3-4) -- The field, the players, the deal

**Figure 1** -- the field at kickoff.

![The field at kickoff](figures/fig-01-the-field.png)

- The three zones and the space codes; home attacks right, the visitors
  attack left; *forward* and *back* point opposite ways for the two
  coaches. (Law 2.1-2.2)
- Shooting range: the dashed band. The one time the book says what you
  cannot do: *you cannot shoot from outside it.* The middle space is in
  neither range. (Law 2.3)
- The kickoff space, and that both sides kick off from it on this board.
  (Law 2.4)
- **The roles** -- *art needed*: the six role cards as a fan, each with
  its two skills and its ability in one line. The text: every player
  has an offensive skill and a defensive skill, and they always total 7;
  every role has one ability. (Law 2.6) A footnote sends the reader to
  the role table on the back page.
- **The standard deal**: 2-2-2 by role, read straight off Figure 1.
  Nine players, six on the field, three on the bench. (Law 3.2)
- **Set up your first game** -- a numbered box: choose board 7; flip
  the coin for home; deal 2-2-2 as in Figure 1; ball on the kickoff
  space at speed 1, home's; clock at 00. Skip the setup Coaching
  Choice for your first game (the Charter offers one, 3.3; you will
  meet it on page 11).

## Spread 2 (pages 5-6) -- A turn, and the six cards

**Figure 4** -- one turn, in order.

![One turn](figures/fig-04-one-turn.png)

- The handler: whoever of yours is standing on the ball; if more than
  one, you choose -- unless the last play left it with somebody in
  particular. (Law 4.2)
- The three things a handler can do: shoot (only in range), maneuver,
  or call a time out. Most turns are maneuvers. (Law 4.3)
- Whoever has the ball when the turn ends takes the next one. (Law 4.1)

**Figure 3** -- the six cards.

![The six cards](figures/fig-03-the-six-cards.png)

- A maneuver is a fight for the ball between two players: your handler
  and one defender. Both coaches choose a card in secret and reveal
  together. (Law 6.1-6.2)
- The offense's three and the defense's three, one line each, straight
  off the cards' own text.

**Figure 2** -- the cycle.

![The cycle](figures/fig-02-the-cycle.png)

- Rank decides, not dice: each card beats one of the other side's, ties
  one and loses to one. (Law 6.3)
- A tie is not a draw. It goes to a **skill test**: d12 + offensive
  skill against d12 + defensive skill, and an exhaustion token to each
  player for the effort. Higher wins and their card is what happens.
  (Law 6.4)
- **Who defends?** A defender already standing on the ball challenges,
  and cannot refuse. Otherwise the defending coach may *send* one of
  their two nearest players to the ball -- one token per space walked --
  or send nobody, and let the maneuver through. (Law 6.1, Law 9)

## Spreads 3-4 (pages 7-10) -- Your first five turns

The board is set up from Figure 1. The reader plays home; a friend, or
the reader's other hand, plays the visitors and plays the card the book
names. Each turn is one column: the position, what both sides play,
what happens, the rule it shows. This is the bot's tutorial opening
turn for turn, so the two teach the same five lessons in the same order.

**Turn 1 -- Figure 5.** Home: Dribble Advance. Visitors: Deflect.

![Beat 1](figures/fig-05-beat-1.png)

- Rank settles it, no dice. The Playmaker's ability: two spaces instead
  of one. You set the ball's speed as you go -- pick anything, it will
  not matter yet. You are now in shooting range. (Law 6.6, 2.6)

**Turn 2 -- Figure 6.** Home: Low Pass. Visitors: Deflect. Rolled: home
loses.

![Beat 2](figures/fig-06-beat-2.png)

- A tie and a skill test, a token each. The Deflect knocks the ball
  back one space -- onto a visitors' player already standing there, so
  it is simply theirs: no roll, and never loose. (Law 6.4, 6.8, 10.1)
- That is a turnover, so everyone outside their own zone runs back,
  one token a space. Your Playmaker walks home. (Law 12.3)
- Margin: *a ball only comes loose on an empty space* (page 11).

**Turn 3 -- Figure 7.** Visitors: Dribble Advance. Home: Pressure.

![Beat 3](figures/fig-07-beat-3.png)

- Nobody of yours is on the ball, so you send a challenger: either of
  your two nearest, one token a space. (Law 9)
- Pressure beats Dribble Advance: their handler and the ball are shoved
  back a space and your challenger follows onto them. They still have
  the ball -- but they have lost ground, and you are standing on it.
  (Law 6.10)
- Margin: *push a handler off the end of the field and they roll for an
  own goal* (Law 11).

**Turn 4 -- Figure 8.** Visitors: Low Pass. Home: Steal.

![Beat 4](figures/fig-08-beat-4.png)

- Steal beats Low Pass: possession flips, the speed resets to 1, your
  stealer carries the ball a space back toward your goal, and everyone
  out of position runs back. (Law 6.9, 12.1, 12.3)
- Then the stealer sets the ball's speed, up to their defensive skill.
  Take the most: speed 4 is worth +2 on the shot you are about to take.
  (Law 6.9, 7.1)

**Turn 5 -- Figure 9.** Home: High Pass, 2 spaces. Visitors: Steal.

![Beat 5](figures/fig-09-beat-5.png)

- High Pass beats Steal. A pass of exactly 2 is caught cleanly; 3 or
  more has to be won. Caught in shooting range, it is a **scoring
  opportunity**: your Striker shoots at once, out of turn, and adds 3
  for it. (Law 6.7, 8.1-8.3)
- The shot: d12 + 6 + 3 + 2 against d12 + 6 for the Fullback standing
  on the ball. Equal totals score. Roll it. (Law 5.2-5.3)
- Whatever happens, the ball is dead: a **new play**. Both sides go
  back to where they started, and the side restarting may take a
  Coaching Choice (page 11). Then keep playing -- you know enough now.
  (Law 5.5, 12.4)

## Spread 5 (pages 11-12) -- Shooting, speed, and where the ball lands

**Figure 10** -- what a shot is up against.

![The shot](figures/fig-10-the-shot.png)

- On the ball, a defender adds their whole defensive skill; between the
  ball and the goal, half, rounded up, each; behind the ball, nothing.
  (Law 5.3)
- A shot costs a minute and nothing else. Goal or miss, it is a new
  play: a goal restarts from the conceding side's kickoff space, a miss
  gives the ball to the defense on their own last space. (Law 5.4-5.5)

**Figure 13** -- ball speed.

![Ball speed](figures/fig-13-ball-speed.png)

- The ball is a d12 and the face it shows is its speed. Half of it,
  rounded down, is added to your shot -- and to a Steal against you.
  Every turnover resets it to 1. (Law 7)

**Figure 11** -- where the ball comes to rest.

![Where the ball lands](figures/fig-11-where-the-ball-lands.png)

- When a play leaves the ball on a space with nobody holding it,
  whoever is standing there decides: nobody, and it is loose -- each
  side may send a player after it, or not; one side, and it is theirs;
  both, and the two already there roll for it. (Law 10.1-10.3)
- If nobody goes after a loose ball it goes out, to the other side, and
  they send somebody to pick it up. (Law 10.5-10.6)

## Spread 6 (pages 13-14) -- Turnovers, the Coaching Choice, tokens and the clock

- **Two kinds of turnover.** A *steal* keeps the ball live: everyone
  runs back to their own zone and play carries on. A *new play* -- a
  goal, a miss, a ball out of bounds -- resets both sides to their
  arrangement and offers the restarting side a Coaching Choice. (Law
  12.1)

**Figure 12** -- the Coaching Choice.

![Coaching Choice](figures/fig-12-coaching-choice.png)

- What you may do in one: change formation, substitute, swap two
  players' zones, move a player within their zone. Two substitutions a
  half. Finish with somebody on your kickoff space. The positions you
  finish on are your *arrangement* -- where a new play puts you back.
  (Law 14.1-14.2, 14.5, 14.8)
- **Time out.** Out of range, once a half, and not in the last minute:
  a minute on the clock buys both coaches a Coaching Choice and the
  ball stays where it is. (Law 13)
- **Exhaustion and injury** -- *art needed*: a player card with tokens
  stacked past its defensive skill, and the two condition markers.
  Tokens come from skill tests, from walking to the ball and from
  running back. More tokens than defensive skill is *Exhausted*; an
  Exhausted player rolls an injury check after every skill test, and a
  roll no higher than their tokens *injures* them. An injured player
  loses ties and adds nothing in a contest; only a substitution takes
  them off. Halftime takes a token off everyone. (Law 15)
- **The clock** -- *art needed*: the jumbotron's clock track with a
  marker on 14. Every maneuver costs a minute, a High Pass two, a shot
  one. The clock never stops. When it reaches 15, whoever has the ball
  has *last possession*: the next turnover ends the half. Halftime
  recovers a token and offers both coaches a Coaching Choice; the
  second half starts at 16 with the visitors kicking off. (Law 16)
- **Full time and the shootout.** Higher score wins. Level, and each
  coach lines up their six in secret and shoots one against one, d12 +
  offense, best of six with sudden death after. (Law 17)

## Page 15 -- Appendix: advanced mode

Two things switch on, and a game may take either without the other.
Everything on this page is Part II of the Charter.

**Figure 14** -- the twelve cards on the cycle.

![The gambits](figures/fig-14-the-gambits.png)

- **Gambits.** Every rank gets a second card: the same maneuver, bigger
  -- and with a price when it is beaten. You hold yours only while your
  team is *behind*: fewer goals, or more Exhausted-or-Injured players
  on the field. A gambit is only played against a challenge. (Law 19)

**Figure 15** -- the four species.

![The species](figures/fig-15-the-species.png)

- **Species abilities.** Fire Demons ignite, Cyborgs run on drain,
  Telekinetics pull the ball, Oozes merge. One line each, and the
  species reference cards in the box carry the rest. (Law 20)

## Page 16 -- Back page: the quick reference

- The role table: six roles, two skills, one ability each. (Law 2.6)
- The rolls: score attempt, skill test, own goal, injury check,
  shootout -- one line each, from Appendix A of the Charter.
- The turn in one line; the two turnovers in one line each.
- The clock costs.
